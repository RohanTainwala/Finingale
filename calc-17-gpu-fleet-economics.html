Terms used

GPU-hour

One GPU working for one hour. Eight GPUs for three hours is 24 GPU-hours. A fleet of 1,000 GPUs has 8,760,000 GPU-hours a year to sell.

Steady-state year

A full year with every GPU installed and every contract running. A contract's first year usually earns less, because installing the GPUs takes several months before billing starts.

Take-or-pay contract

A customer reserves a block of GPUs for a fixed term and pays for every hour whether they use it or not. It removes occupancy risk for those GPUs and replaces it with customer credit risk and renewal risk at the end of the term.

Contract price per GPU-hour

A contract's total value divided by its length, the number of GPUs it covers and 8,760 hours a year. If the contract also covers storage or services, part of this price pays for those.

Occupancy

The share of hours an on-demand GPU is actually rented out. An empty hour still carries its depreciation, rent and overheads, so low occupancy pushes up the cost of every hour that does sell.

Resale uplift

Some contracts let the provider resell the customer's reserved capacity to others at a higher price. The uplift is the margin above the contract price; the provider usually keeps only part of it.

Customer prepayment

Cash a contract customer pays at signing, used to place the GPU order. It reduces the capital the provider funds and borrows. It is not extra income: it is part of the contract value, received early.

Depreciation

The purchase price of the GPUs spread evenly over the years they are expected to earn money, less any resale value. It is not a cash payment each year, but it is a real cost: the money was spent upfront and has to be earned back.

EBITDA

Earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation. Revenue minus cash operating costs. It shows how much cash the fleet generates before paying back the capital and the lenders.

Payback

Years of cash earnings (after interest) needed to recover the capital the provider funded. Payback longer than the GPUs' useful life means they wear out before they have paid for themselves.

Price cushion

How far revenue could fall, across every price, before profit before tax reaches zero. A negative cushion is how much revenue would need to rise to break even.

Revenue per MW

Annual revenue divided by the fleet's power capacity in megawatts. Data-centre capacity is secured in MW, so this shows how much each MW earns and makes fleets of different sizes comparable.

What unit should GPU costs be measured in?

The GPU-hour: one GPU working for one hour. Customers pay per GPU-hour, and a provider's finance team should express every cost per available GPU-hour, so revenue and cost sit on the same unit. Capacity is a second unit alongside it: data-centre space is bought in megawatts, which caps how many GPU-hours there are to sell.

Why not tokens or energy?

Tokens are one layer further from the hardware. A company running AI models on rented GPUs may charge its own users per token, but the GPU provider never sees tokens in its billing: it sells GPU time.

Energy is a real cost but not the main one.

How to standardise GPU costs

  1. Pick one base unit. Express every cost per available GPU-hour, so depreciation, rent, power and overheads add up on the same basis.
  2. Use effective prices, not list prices. Convert each contract to a price per GPU-hour from its total value, term and GPU count, after discounts, prepayments and any resale uplift.
  3. Adjust for GPU type. An hour on an older GPU does far less work than an hour on the newest one. Compare different models using a reference GPU, measured on your own workloads.
  4. Track utilisation separately. Report cost per available hour and cost per billed hour side by side. The gap is the cost of capacity that sat empty.
  5. Load in every cost. Include storage, networking, support and financing, not only the GPU and its power, so the unit cost is complete.
  6. Report capacity in MW. Tie GPU-hour economics back to the MW of data-centre capacity they use, because that is how capacity is bought and how lenders and investors compare providers.
