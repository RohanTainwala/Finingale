The five setups

Every rule uses daily closing prices only. A stock can match more than one setup. Only stocks priced at $5 or more that traded at least 1,000,000 shares in their last session are scanned.

20-day breakout

Closing-high breakout

The latest close is above every close of the 20 sessions before it.

Bull flag

Run-up, then a pause

Up more than 10% over 10 sessions, then the last 5 closes held inside a 5% range.

Yesterday's close was below its 20-day moving average; the latest close is above it.

First up day

Do the setups work?

We tested every signal from January 2025 to October 2026 across about 1,500 liquid US stocks, comparing each one with the average stock over the same days. The short answer: not reliably.

≈ 0%

Breakouts, bull flags and 20-day reclaims did no better than the average stock over the next 1, 5 or 10 days, and beat it less than half the time.

+0.7%

Oversold stocks averaged about 0.7% better over 10 days, but beat the average only 49.7% of the time. A few big rebounds carry the number, and it swung widely month to month.

Costs

The test ignores trading costs and stocks that were delisted. Both would make the real-world results worse.

About this data

The scan runs automatically after each US trading session, using that day's closing prices. The stock list comes from Nasdaq Trader's public symbol directories; prices come from Yahoo Finance's free charts, which are unofficial and can lag or contain errors. Always check prices with your broker.

Past patterns do not predict future returns. Most people who trade short-term lose money. Nothing on this page is financial advice.
