The five setups

Each rule uses daily closing prices only, and a stock can match more than one. The sketches show the shape each rule looks for.

20-day breakout

Closing-high breakout

The latest close is above every close of the 20 sessions before it.

Bull flag

Run-up, then a pause

Up more than 10% over 10 sessions, then the last 5 closes held inside a 5% range.

Reclaimed 20-day avg

Back above the average

Yesterday's close was below its 20-day moving average, and the latest close is above it.

Oversold

RSI under 30

The 14-day Relative Strength Index has dropped below 30 after a sharp fall.

Oversold bounce

The first up day

RSI was under 30 the session before, and the latest close was higher.

Do the setups actually work?

We tested every signal from January 2025 to October 2026 across about 1,500 liquid US stocks, comparing each one with the average stock over the same days. The short answer: not reliably.

≈ 0%

Breakouts, bull flags and 20-day reclaims did no better than the average stock over the next 1, 5 or 10 days, and beat it less than half the time.

+0.7%

Oversold stocks averaged about 0.7% better over 10 days but beat the average only 49.7% of the time. A few big rebounds carry the number, and it swung widely month to month.

Costs

The test ignores trading costs and stocks that were later delisted. Both would make real-world results worse.

About this data

How it updates

The scan runs on its own after every US trading session, using that day's closing prices, and this page shows the result the next morning, Australian time. On US market holidays the list stays on the last trading day.

The stock list comes from Nasdaq Trader's public symbol directories. Only stocks priced at $5 or more that traded at least 1,000,000 shares in their last session are scanned.

What to keep in mind

Prices come from Yahoo Finance's free charts, which are unofficial and can lag or contain errors. Always check prices with your broker.

Past patterns do not predict future returns, and most people who trade short-term lose money. Nothing on this page is financial advice.
